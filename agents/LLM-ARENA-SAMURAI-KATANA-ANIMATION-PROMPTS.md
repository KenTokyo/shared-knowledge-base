# LLM Arena prompts — Samurai Katana animation directions

Use the same original character, katana, scabbard, six skills, target marker, stage, camera, controls, timing, and VFX rules in every run. Only the animation method changes. Compare readable body mechanics, foot plants, blade arcs, root travel, airborne rotation, hit timing, transitions, recovery, and identical replay stability. Fast must still look physical: every dash needs a launch and stop, every flip needs a takeoff and landing, and every hit needs a visible blade path.

## Variation 1 — Code-authored keyframe clips

```text
Build a compact Three.js animation showcase with one original, photorealistic dark-fantasy Samurai class: a fast katana duelist with the explosive mobility and combo density of a top-tier action-MMO ninja, without copying an existing character, costume, animation, or weapon. Give the fighter a sharp combat silhouette, a sheathed katana on the left hip, disciplined two-handed handling when power matters, and stylish one-handed control only when the move requires it. Use a fixed three-quarter gameplay camera on a dark moonlit dojo arena. Keep the full body, both feet, katana, scabbard, travel path, and landing point visible. Add a simple humanoid target marker for distance and contact reads. Do not hide movement with teleports, cuts, or camera shake.

Create every move in code with AnimationClip, QuaternionKeyframeTrack, VectorKeyframeTrack, and AnimationMixer. Key the root, hips, spine, shoulders, arms, hands, legs, feet, katana, and scabbard interaction. Each skill needs readable anticipation, launch, root travel, blade contact, recoil or follow-through, stable landing, and guard recovery. Preserve shortest-path quaternion rotation, believable center-of-mass shifts, planted support feet, exact blade orientation, and clean draw and re-sheathe alignment. Use named timeline events for draw, dash launch, each hit, landing, detonation, and recovery. Never let feet skate, knees collapse, hands detach from the grip, or the blade clip through the body and scabbard.

Add six skills on keys 1–6. 1, Voidflash Iaido: thumb the guard, sink low, burst eight meters through the target, complete one horizontal draw cut, stop in a wide stance, then trigger a delayed razor-line burst before re-sheathing. 2, Crescent Vault: diagonal sidestep dash, low shin-level sweep, plant one hand, vault into a forward flip over the target, then land behind it with a reverse rising cut; make all three hits distinct. 3, Dragon Step — Sevenfold Path: chain seven readable hits through left-right zigzag dashes—draw cut, reverse waist cut, shoulder feint, rising diagonal, turning back cut, knee-height pass, and two-handed finishing cleave—with tiny pose holds after hits three and six so the combo does not become visual mush. 4, Tempest Wheel: sprint laterally, enter a one-handed aerial cartwheel, carve two circular cuts during the rotation, land on one knee, and release a final ground-skimming crescent. 5, Skyfang Pursuit: launch with a rising diagonal cut, chase upward, complete one clear forward somersault, strike down during the last quarter-turn, and drive the target line into the floor with a four-hit aerial combo. 6, Eclipse Execution: backflip away from danger, land in a silent sheath stance, fire four cross-arena iai dashes from alternating angles, corkscrew upward, then meteor-plunge with a two-handed vertical cleave; after a short freeze, detonate the stored slash grid in one controlled finale.

Make dashes fast but spatially traceable, flips fully readable, and combo contacts evenly staged. Add high-impact steel-blue blade ribbons, compressed wind cones, white sparks, thin shock rings, floor-cut lines, and a restrained crimson accent for Eclipse Execution. Build every visible effect with hand-written GLSL and runtime-generated geometry only—no effect textures, sprites, flipbooks, videos, imported VFX meshes, packages, or copied shaders. Sample the final evaluated katana edge path once and drive trails, sparks, cut lines, light, and contact bursts from that shared path plus exact timeline events. Keep the fighter visible through every effect. Use fixed-capacity pools and reset every animation, root transform, trail sample, event flag, and VFX slot between replays. Space replays the current skill; R performs a full deterministic reset.
```
